01 / Context

The problem

A coaching website needs to move beyond an introduction: collect useful context, prepare a plan, let the trainer review it, and give members a place to track their training.

02 / Ownership

My contribution

I built the responsive website and guided assessment, server-side validation, a local personalized-plan engine, branded PDFs, email workflows, and trainer review tools. The member experience adds email-code sign-in, published programs, workout logs, daily check-ins, progress charts, and private photos.

  1. Collect and validate the member’s goal, schedule, and equipment.
  2. Generate a local draft plan and branded PDF for trainer review.
  3. Deliver the reviewed plan; publish approved programs in the member portal.

03 / Engineering

Decisions behind the interface

Generate locally, then review

The assessment workflow generates a draft without a paid AI request. The PDF goes to the trainer for review before the finished plan is sent to the customer.

Separate drafts from published programs

Member-program edits can be saved privately. Only trainer-published versions appear to the member, and published versions remain immutable.

Treat member data as private application data

D1 stores the member records, while private R2 storage holds photos and files. Photo access is limited to the member or authenticated trainer; schema changes use Drizzle migrations.

04 / Outcome

What the project delivers

A live public coaching site is connected to the assessment and review workflow, with a private portal for ongoing member activity. The public homepage can be explored directly.
